# 5.2 Thermal Output and Singularity Dissipation

The secondary physical cost manifests as extreme thermal energy generated by the singularity knot at the center of the working volume. As Subsystem BB-2 ramps the asymptotic gravitational pull during Phase P4, the compression of spatial metrics around the central focus releases significant metabolic and thermodynamic heat. This thermal output scales continuously with the primary radius **R** and the address density parameter (δ). By the start of soma printing at Phase P6, the mid kekkai shell reaches intense thermal saturation, rendering the immediate perimeter hazardous to unshielded personnel.

To prevent heat dissipation from destabilizing the ten inward printing heads on the mid wall, the containment architecture must maintain rigid structural insulation. The Outer Wall must remain fully sealed to prevent atmospheric convective currents from disturbing the interior print volume. If ambient thermal energy spikes beyond the dissipation limit of the singularity sink (BB-3), the knot risks overdraw bleed (F3). Under these conditions, the knot consumes its own local address coordinates to cool the core, introducing severe structural flaws into the soma print before dermal closure can be initiated.
