# 14.1 Dual-Caster Operational Architecture and Shared Persistence Mechanics

The execution of high-tier **Anastasis workings** introduces severe operational stress on the primary caster's personal time continuity reserve. Subsystem BB-9 establishes that the artificial persistence required to maintain an abstract Form in physical space cannot be drawn from physical life force or ambient energy without destabilizing the half-index state inside the kekkai boundary. Under standard single-caster protocols, the persistence debt is extracted entirely from the primary caster's personal consciousness, consuming thirty to forty minutes of subjective temporal awareness. When spatial volumes expand or pull durations extend, this single-draw mechanism risks pushing the primary caster into total temporal depletion. Dual-caster continuity anchoring resolves this boundary constraint by establishing a parallel, multi-channel anchor bridge across the outer kekkai layer.

In a dual-caster configuration, the secondary practitioner does not participate in active spellcasting, address recitation, or kekkai modulation. Instead, the secondary caster functions exclusively as a dedicated continuity reservoir, standing in a passive, high-receptivity state directly adjacent to the primary draw vector outside the outer wall line. By coupling their temporal awareness into Subsystem BB-9 prior to the execution of Phase P6, the secondary caster creates a shared draw capacity. This dynamic arrangement allows artificial persistence debt to be divided proportionally across both participants, effectively doubling the operational continuity ceiling and preventing excessive memory degradation or identity fatigue in high-load scenarios.
