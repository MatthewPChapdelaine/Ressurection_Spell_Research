# 8.3 Witness Co-Recitation Mechanics and Distance Anchoring

The inclusion of a living witness during Phase P1 is not merely a legal requirement; it is a vital structural component of the address resolution system. While the primary caster executes the physical drawing and vocalizes the main verses, the witness must deliver the response during the sixth verse. Standing precisely beyond the outer wall line, the witness provides an external relational benchmark that prevents the address lattice from drifting inward toward the caster's own personal coordinates.

The witness must speak their response with clear, unhesitating projection. If the witness stumbles or fails to vocalize the sixth differential at the exact moment the caster completes the corresponding vertex line, the address lattice suffers immediate reference loss. Without a valid external witness anchor, the retrieval vector loses directional stability, dramatically increasing the risk of near-neighbor capture (F1). Maintaining absolute synchronization between the caster's primary chant and the witness's vocal response guarantees that the search grid remains accurately calibrated against external physical reality.
