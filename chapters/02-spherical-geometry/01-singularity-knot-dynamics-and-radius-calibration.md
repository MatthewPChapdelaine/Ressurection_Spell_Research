# 2.1 Singularity Knot Dynamics and Radius Calibration

The establishment of a stable spherical containment field begins with the precise placement of the **singularity knot** at the origin point of the working space. This knot does not serve as a material anchor; rather, it functions as a localized point of absolute topological compression. By folding the surrounding metaphysical metric inward, the singularity knot creates a fixed spatial focal point toward which the target Form can be asymptotically pulled. Establishing this center point requires absolute stillness in the ambient energy field, as any lateral drift during the early setup phase will distort the overall spherical symmetry of the outer containment shells.

Once the singularity knot is anchored, the primary radius **R** must be calculated. A common operational error involves measuring the physical dimensions of the subject's coffin or resting place. In the Anastasis framework, physical containers are entirely irrelevant. Radius **R** must be derived strictly from the **remembered silhouette span** held within the residual curvature of the subject's biological remains. Practitioners measure the widest geometric extent of the target Form's posture during its fully realized physical state, adding a mandatory safety clearance margin to accommodate spatial variance during the instantiation sequence.

For a standard adult human Form, the nominal calibrated radius **R** is precisely 1.9 meters. Scaling the geometry below a minimum threshold of 1.4 meters introduces severe metric pinch. If radius **R** is compressed under 1.4 meters, the inner spatial volume becomes insufficient to contain the expanding topological features of the Form during retrieval. This compression causes structural shearing along the outer edges of the pattern, leading to permanent spatial distortion or complete retrieval failure before the soma printing phase can even begin.
