# 1.3 Core Doctrine 2: Form Persistence and Kekkai Boundaries

The second claim (D2) addresses the fundamental instability of retrieved Forms outside their native domain. Forms do not possess inherent physical persistence because persistence is strictly a property of physical matter. The moment an abstract pattern is retrieved into the physical realm, it immediately begins to degrade and slip unless artificially anchored. This reality makes the spherical kekkai boundary an essential structural component rather than a decorative ward. The kekkai must supply continuous, artificial persistence across three anisotropic shells throughout the retrieval, printing, and binding phases. If the outer containment field collapses prematurely, the system does not release a living person; instead, it dissipates an abstract *description* into the surrounding environment.
