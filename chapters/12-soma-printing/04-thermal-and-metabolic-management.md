# 12.4 Thermal and Metabolic Management

Rapid tissue synthesis generates extreme metabolic and thermodynamic heat within the mid kekkai shell. As cellular structures materialize at high speed, the conversion of substrate energy into physical mass releases intense thermal radiation, causing ambient temperatures inside the print volume to rise rapidly. If unmanaged, this heat buildup threatens to warp the projection alignment of the ten inward heads or induce thermal overdraw bleed (F3) at the central singularity knot.

To prevent heat damage during tissue synthesis, Subsystem BB-3 operates a continuous thermal dissipation sink that absorbs excess metabolic energy from the interior air volume. The primary caster must monitor internal thermal output continuously throughout Phase P6, making precise adjustments to the address density parameter (δ) if temperature spikes occur. Managing thermal stress guarantees that cellular assembly proceeds without heat-induced deformation, allowing the completed soma to reach dermal seam closure with total structural stability.
