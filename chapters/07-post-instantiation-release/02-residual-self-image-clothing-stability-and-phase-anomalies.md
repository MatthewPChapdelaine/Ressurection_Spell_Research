# 7.2 Residual Self-Image Clothing Stability and Phase Anomalies

During the soma printing sequence executed by Subsystem BB-7, garments and personal attire are synthesized directly alongside dermal tissue, drawing structural metrics from the subject's Residual Self-Image (RSI) cache. Consequently, the clothing worn by the returned individual upon release is not woven physical fabric; it is printed soma matter structured to mimic the appearance, texture, and flexibility of everyday garments. Under dry, normal atmospheric conditions, RSI clothing performs identically to ordinary textiles, providing proper thermal protection and natural movement.

However, printed RSI clothing exhibits a severe phase anomaly when exposed to moisture. If the garments become wet from rain, water immersion, or heavy sweat, the underlying cellular alignment shifts, causing the material to behave like living dermal tissue for approximately sixty minutes. During this phase transition, the fabric becomes semi-permeable, sensitive, and bound to the subject's nervous system. To prevent discomfort, psychological shock, or physical irritation during post-instantiation transfer, operational protocols require providing the returned individual with genuine textile outerwear immediately after they clear the containment field.
